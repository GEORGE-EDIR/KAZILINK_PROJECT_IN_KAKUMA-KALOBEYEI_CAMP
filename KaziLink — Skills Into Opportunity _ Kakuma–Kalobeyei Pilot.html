● 01 / Problem Statement & Target Group

Potential is present. Access is the missing link.

Many young people in Kakuma and Kalobeyei have relevant abilities, yet face four structural barriers when entering employment.

01
Limited Professional Networks Youth often lack direct connections to mentors, references, and hiring partners.
02
Few Practical Experience Opportunities Structured fellowships, returnships, and work-based assignments are scarce.
03
Difficulty Demonstrating Skills Employers often rely solely on formal work history or personal referrals.
04
Unreliable Pathway Information Reliable guidance on training, placements, and available jobs is hard to navigate.
TARGET PARTICIPANTS

50 Youth Aged 18–30
Refugee & Host Communities

  • 01 Young people seeking their first employment opportunity
  • 02 Individuals with skills but limited formal work experience
  • 03 Graduates or trainees seeking practical work exposure
  • 04 People returning to work after a period away from employment
✓ Selection will be transparent, inclusive, and guided by clear eligibility criteria.

● 03 / Main Project Activities

Five integrated building blocks for youth readiness.

Combining human mentorship, safe practical assignments, and consent-driven skills matching.

ACTIVITY 01

Participant Outreach & Skills Assessment

  • Share project information through community networks and local organizations
  • Invite applications using simple and accessible methods
  • Assess participants' interests, existing skills, and career goals
  • Create a baseline record of skills and employment needs
ACTIVITY 02

Mentorship

  • Recruit and orient volunteer or paid mentors
  • Match participants and mentors based on interests, skills, and goals
  • Hold regular mentorship sessions
  • Support participants to develop practical career plans
ACTIVITY 03

Practical Experience

  • Identify short, supervised assignments with partner organizations
  • Set clear expectations and learning objectives for each placement
  • Provide feedback and a record of completed work
  • Ensure placements are safe, fair, and do not replace paid staff
ACTIVITY 04

Skills-Based Profiles & Matching

  • Help participants create profiles showing their skills, projects, training, and experience
  • Use a simple digital tool or database to organize profiles and opportunities
  • Match participants to opportunities based on stated skill requirements
  • Obtain informed consent before sharing participant information with employers
ACTIVITY 05

Employer & Community Partnerships

  • Engage local businesses, NGOs, community organizations, and training providers
  • Identify opportunities for interviews, projects, internships, fellowships, or paid work
  • Gather ongoing feedback from partners and participants to improve the project
● 04 / Proposed Six-Month Work Plan

Start small. Learn together.

A phased six-month schedule moving from community consultation and setup to mentorship, assignments, and end-of-pilot evaluation.

MONTH 01

Consultation & Setup

Community consultation, partner outreach, project setup, and participant recruitment.

MONTH 02

Assess & Orient

Skills assessments, mentor recruitment, and participant orientation.

MONTH 03

Mentorship & Profiles

Mentorship begins; participant skills-based profiles are developed.

MONTH 04

Assignments & Matching

Practical assignments and skills-based matching begin.

MONTH 05

Employer Engagement

Employer engagement, interviews, and continued mentorship.

MONTH 06

Review & Next Phase

Follow-up, evaluation, partner review, and planning for the next phase.

● 06 / Monitoring & Evaluation

Tracking progress and learning from every step.

KaziLink will track progress through a short baseline assessment, monthly activity records, and an end-of-pilot review.

STAGE 1 · BASELINE

Short Baseline Assessment

Captures participants' starting skills, interests, employment needs, and career goals at entry.

STAGE 2 · ONGOING

Monthly Activity Records

Logs participation, mentorship sessions completed, assignments, referrals, and emerging challenges.

STAGE 3 · ENDLINE

End-of-Pilot Review

Assesses verified work outcomes alongside participant and partner feedback to guide future phases.

CORE INDICATORS TRACKED ACROSS THE PILOT
01 Number of participants recruited and completing activities
02 Number of mentors and partners engaged
03 Mentorship sessions completed
04 Practical assignments and referrals provided
05 Interviews, paid assignments, and employment outcomes
06 Participant and partner qualitative feedback
● 07 / Key Risks & Safeguards

Opportunity must be safe, fair, and accountable.

Proactive safeguards built into participant onboarding, data privacy, and workplace placements.

Identified Pilot Risk KaziLink Proposed Response & Safeguard
Limited employer opportunities Start with a small number of realistic partners (target: 5) and expand gradually.
Digital access barriers Offer offline or assisted registration and profile support alongside digital tools.
Participant data privacy Collect only necessary information and obtain informed consent before sharing.
Unfair or unsafe placements Use written expectations, supervision, safeguarding procedures, and fair compensation where applicable.
Low mentor availability Recruit a diverse mentor pool (target: 15) and set clear, realistic time commitments.
